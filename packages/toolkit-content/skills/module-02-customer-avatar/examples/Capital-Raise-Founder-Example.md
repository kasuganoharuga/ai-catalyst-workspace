## Venture
- Venture name: Capital raise program (example)

## Segment

Australian pre-seed / seed founders raising $500k–$3M.

## Snapshot

**WHO:** 32–42, technical or domain-expert founder; 2–8 person team

**WHERE:** Sydney / Melbourne / Brisbane. Often accelerator-adjacent

**STAGE:** Post-MVP, $10k–$80k ARR or strong pilots. 6–12 mths runway

**RAISE / CURRENT COMMERCIAL MOMENT:** First institutional round. SAFE, note or priced seed

## Situation

Has proven the product works and now needs capital to hire and scale — but the raise itself is a
black box. Has taken ~15 coffee meetings that went nowhere and redesigned the deck four times,
without knowing whether the problem is the deck, the story, the targeting or the timing. Running
the raise while running the company, and both are suffering.

## Unmet Needs

### Functional — what they need done

1. Close the round in a defined window (12–16 weeks), not an open-ended drift that burns 9 months of runway.
2. Avoid mistakes that cost them control — stacked SAFEs, uncapped notes, over-dilution, early board seats, mis-built ESOP.
3. Learn the process end-to-end so it's repeatable at Series A. Wants capability, not a done-for-you service.
4. Know who to actually talk to — a qualified list of AU angels, syndicates and pre-seed funds in their sector and cheque size.
5. A defensible number — how much, at what valuation, backed by a model that survives scrutiny.
6. Momentum mechanics — run a process that creates competitive tension, not sequential rejections.

### Emotional and social — what they feel

1. Stop feeling like an outsider. Suspects there's a game being played that nobody has explained.
2. Certainty over vibes. Wants a checklist and a system, not "it depends" from advisors who disagree.
3. Protect their credibility. Terrified of burning their best 20 investor relationships with a premature approach.
4. Not be alone in it. Rejection with no feedback loop is corrosive. Wants peers in the same trench.
5. Be seen running a tight, professional process by their co-founder, team and early backers.
6. Make the announcement a credibility event, not just a funding event.

## Buying Signals

### Tier 1 — high intent, act within 24–48 hours

- Searches: "capital raising program / course Australia", "how to raise a seed round", "how much equity to give away pre-seed", "SAFE vs convertible note AU", "seed valuation Australia".
- Downloads the AICD capital raising director tool, the Cake Equity AU raise guide, or the Stone & Chalk white paper — two or more in a 14-day window is the single strongest signal.
- Grabs a term sheet template, cap table model or data room checklist.
- Says it out loud: posts "we're kicking off our seed raise", or asks a founder group "what's a fair pre-seed valuation right now?"

### Tier 2 — building intent, nurture over 4–12 weeks

- Trigger events: first paying customers, first sales hire, went full-time, company registered 6–24 months ago.
- Accepted into or just graduated an accelerator — demo day creates a hard raise deadline.
- Applied for R&D Tax Incentive or a grant; appointed an advisor or non-exec director.
- Signed up to cap table tooling; follows AU VC partners; attends investor nights at Stone & Chalk / Fishburners.
- Consumes 3+ pieces of our content in a week — valuation, dilution and investor targeting convert hardest.

## Disqualifiers

- Wants a broker to raise it for them.
- Already has a signed term sheet.
- Series A+ with a prior successful round.
- Idea stage, pre-MVP.
- Seeking grants or debt, not equity.

## Core Promise

Run a professional seed raise in a defined window, keep control of your company, and own the process
for next time. They are buying certainty and a sequence — not information. Information is free and
they are already drowning in it.
